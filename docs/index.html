Patient journey by pathway

Properties of a good biomarker and how a cut-off is set

A good biomarker

  • Has a stated purpose: triage, or confirmation
  • Is analytically robust: precise, stable in handling, standardised between laboratories
  • Is validated against PET, CSF or autopsy
  • Separates groups by far more than assay noise
  • Holds up across age, kidney function, ethnicity and comorbidity
  • Changes decisions, and is acceptable and affordable

Setting a cut-off

  1. Measure the marker in people whose amyloid status is known
  2. Choose the cut-off for the purpose, not for maximum accuracy alone
  3. Fix it, then test it in a separate cohort

Why two cut-offs

  • A lower cut-off chosen for sensitivity rules Alzheimer’s out
  • An upper cut-off chosen for specificity rules it in
  • Results in between go on to CSF or PET

Standards: triage ≥90% sensitivity; confirmation ≥90% sensitivity and specificity; uncertain zone ≤20% (Schindler et al. Nat Rev Neurol 2024; Alzheimer’s Association guideline 2025).
